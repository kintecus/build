# Routing Rubrics

Confidence dimensions and thresholds for each ideation track. Each dimension is scored 0-20. Threshold to proceed varies by track - early-stage tracks need less certainty.

## Exploring (threshold: 70/100)

Early-stage ideas need less rigor. You're validating whether to invest more thought, not planning execution.

| Dimension | What to assess |
|-----------|---------------|
| Problem signal | Is there a real problem here, or just a solution looking for one? |
| Who cares | Can we identify who would benefit and why? |
| Hypothesis | Is there a testable belief about why this would work? |
| Assumptions | Are the key assumptions identified (not validated, just named)? |
| Differentiation | Why this and not the existing alternatives? |

**Question focus**: "What makes you think this is a real problem?" and "What would need to be true for this to work?"

**Don't ask about**: Scope boundaries, acceptance criteria, phasing, technical approach. Too early.

## Shaping (threshold: 80/100)

The problem is real. Now figuring out the right approach and appetite.

| Dimension | What to assess |
|-----------|---------------|
| Problem clarity | Do we understand the problem well enough to solve it? |
| Appetite | How much time/effort is this worth? What's the constraint? |
| Solution direction | Is there a rough approach, even if details are fuzzy? |
| Rabbit holes | Are the risky unknowns identified? |
| Boundaries | What's explicitly out? What do we say no to? |

**Question focus**: "If you only had two weeks, what would you build?" and "What's the scariest unknown here?"

**Don't ask about**: Detailed requirements, user stories, non-functional requirements. Too detailed.

## Specifying (threshold: 90/100)

Approach is chosen. Now detailing requirements for execution.

| Dimension | What to assess |
|-----------|---------------|
| Problem clarity | Crystal clear problem with measurable impact. |
| Goal definition | Specific, measurable goals. |
| Success criteria | Testable acceptance criteria for all goals. |
| Scope boundaries | Clear in/out with rationale. |
| Consistency | No contradictions in requirements. |

**Question focus**: "How will you know this is done?" and "What's explicitly not part of this?"

**This is the full rigor track.** All dimensions need to be strong.

## Deciding (threshold: 75/100)

Stuck between options. Need structured comparison.

| Dimension | What to assess |
|-----------|---------------|
| Options clarity | Are the options well-defined and distinct? |
| Criteria | What matters for this decision? What are the tradeoffs? |
| Constraints | What limits the decision space? |
| Reversibility | How reversible is this? One-way door or two-way door? |
| Stakeholders | Who needs to be aligned? Who has veto power? |

**Question focus**: "What would make you choose A over B?" and "What's the cost of being wrong?"

**Don't ask about**: Detailed requirements. Focus on the decision, not the execution.

## Communicating (threshold: 75/100)

Idea exists. Needs to be packaged for an audience.

| Dimension | What to assess |
|-----------|---------------|
| Audience | Who is reading this? What do they care about? |
| Core message | What's the one thing they need to take away? |
| Evidence | What supports the argument? Data, examples, precedent? |
| Ask | What do you want from the audience? Approval? Budget? Alignment? |
| Objections | What pushback will you get? How do you address it? |

**Question focus**: "What's the one thing they need to walk away believing?" and "What will they push back on?"

**Don't ask about**: Technical details unless the audience is technical.
