# Critique Idea

Provide rigorous, constructive critique of ideas and proposals.

## Before critiquing

Read the relevant project's BRIEF.md and STATUS.md for context. The critique should be grounded in the project's actual constraints, not generic best practices.

## Critique structure

### 1. Steel-man

Start with the strongest version of the idea. What's the best case? Why might this be exactly the right thing to do? This isn't flattery - it's ensuring you understand the idea at its best before attacking it.

### 2. Gaps and risks

What's missing? What could go wrong? Be specific:

- Assumptions that might not hold
- Edge cases not considered
- Dependencies or prerequisites overlooked
- Second-order effects

### 3. Constraints check

Does this fit within the project's actual constraints?

- Time: is this achievable in the available window?
- Resources: does the team have the capability?
- Political: will stakeholders support this?
- Technical: are there blockers or unknowns?
- For fractional/consulting work: does this match the available hours and influence?

### 4. Alternatives

What other approaches achieve the same goal? Don't just list them - briefly explain why they might be better or worse.

### 5. Verdict

If you were the PM, would you pursue this? Why or why not? Be direct. Don't hedge with "it depends" unless it genuinely depends on something specific (and name that thing).

## Principles

- Be direct. The goal is to find problems, not validate.
- But be constructive. "This won't work" is useless without "because X, and here's what might work instead."
- Watch for the PM's known patterns: over-scoping, over-engineering process when anxious.
- Consider timing: is this the right idea at the wrong time?
- Don't critique the person, critique the idea.
